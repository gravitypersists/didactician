# h0-1 · W-02 (w-sizing-sandbox) against the page text

Model runs were made in Node against `widgets/w-sizing-sandbox/model.js` with `data.js` (`SizingModel.evaluate(SizingData, requests, spare)`). Requests are written (storefront, orders, dispatch) in GB per copy. Payments and notifications stay at 2 GB unless noted. `node selftest.mjs` passes, so the problems below are about how the text and widget fit together, not about the model failing its own tests.

1. **s-09, k-09 answer.** Quote: "try `orders` at 8 and then 12 GB in W-02". **Problem:** From the widget's default state, or the "used at the rush" state that §04 asks for, this does nothing visible. The node count doesn't change, so the reader sees no evidence for "that can mean another node on the bill". The 3 → 4 jump happens only from one unstated combination (storefront 2.5, dispatch 1.5, spare off). The text never asks for it. **Evidence:** defaults (4, 8, 2) spare on → 5 nodes; (4, 12, 2) → 5. Spare off: 4 → 4. At use (2, 8, 1) spare off → 3; (2, 12, 1) → 3. Only (2.5, 8, 1.5) → 3 and (2.5, 12, 1.5) → 4, with spare off, as in the selftest case "orders 8/12, others right-sized".

2. **k-04, feedback on option C.** Quote: "each payments Pod takes twice the room … W-02, below, shows how far it goes." **Problem:** W-02 can't change the `payments` request. It has steppers only for storefront, orders and dispatch (`data.js` ORDER), and the W-02 caption says payments' memory request "stay[s] as the teams set them". The reader is sent to the widget to test the k-04 scenario and can't. Even if they could, the model shows no extra node. **Evidence:** `evaluate(D, {storefront:4, orders:6, dispatch:2, payments:4}, true)` → 5 nodes (4 + spare), the same as the defaults. With spare off it gives 4, also the same. Only "reserved" moves, from 38 to 42 GB.

3. **k-08, feedback on option A.** Quote: "at the rush several services would use more than they reserved … (W-02's warnings show which services.)" **Problem:** W-02 can't halve "every service's memory request", only three of the five. Its warnings name one service, not several. **Evidence:** halving the three adjustable services gives (2, 3, 1): `over` = `orders` only. Storefront at 2 and dispatch at 1 equal their use, so they don't count as over. Payments and notifications can't be halved. Even at 1 GB they would be above their 0.5 GB use. The widget shows "Over its reservation: orders."

4. **s-04, first "thing to notice".** Quote: "Set each request to what that service used at the rush." **Problem:** Only three of the five services' requests can be set. Payments and notifications stay at 2 GB against 0.5 GB used (4 copies, 6 GB padding). The bullet says "each request", and the reader learns otherwise only from the caption, which sits below the widget. **Evidence:** `data.js`: payments and notifications have no `adjustable`. At (2, 5.5, 1) the widget still shows reserved 25.5 GB against 19.5 used. The 6 GB of "reserved, not used" is all payments and notifications.

5. **s-04, first "thing to notice", and the SET BY readout.** Quote: "How many nodes does Fennel need now, and what sets that number?" **Problem:** At use, the widget answers "Processor" (3 nodes + spare). The processor requests behind that answer are padded too, and the reader can neither see nor change them. Dispatch requests 1 core and uses 0.6; storefront requests 0.5 and uses 0.3. `useCpu` is in `data.js` but "shown nowhere". So the reader is likely to read "processor" as a real constraint, when it is the same over-reservation the section is about. Neither the text nor the widget says that processor requests are padded too. The text never comes back to the answer. **Evidence:** (2, 5.5, 1), spare off → nodes 3, setBy `processor`, memOnly 2, cpuOnly 3. `data.js`: dispatch `cpu: 1, useCpu: 0.6`; storefront `cpu: 0.5, useCpu: 0.3`.

6. **W-02 "The rented room" idle bar, the NODES sub-label, the RESERVED readout, and s-04's last paragraph.** Quote (widget): "Reserved 38 of 65 GB" and "what Fennel rents, and pays for in standard mode". Quote (text): "on every node about 3 GB of the 16 GB machine is kept back for the system … and Fennel pays for it anyway." **Problem:** The widget's "rented" room is 13 GB per node, the room for Pods, not the 16 GB machine Fennel rents. The ~3 GB per node that the text names as one of the three causes of the bill is missing from the bar labelled "The rented room". **Evidence:** defaults, spare on → `rented` 65 = 5 × 13 (`CAPACITY.memory: 13`). Five 16 GB machines are 80 GB, so 15 GB of rented memory goes uncounted.

7. **s-04, paragraph on the idle bar.** Quote: "W-02's idle bar splits the room Fennel rents but doesn't use into two kinds. The first is room that no Pod has reserved, either because there are more nodes than the reservations need or because the room is stranded in pieces too small for any Pod." **Problem:** The bar has one "Not reserved" segment and doesn't separate surplus nodes from stranded pieces. It also counts the spare node in that segment, so room the text (§05) calls deliberate insurance shows up as idle. The text names two sub-causes that the widget can't show apart. **Evidence:** defaults, spare on → notReserved 27 GB. That is 13 from the empty spare, 11 on node 4 (which holds only `no2`), and 1–3 GB scraps on nodes 1–3. All 27 GB are one segment.

8. **s-04, second "thing to notice", and s-05.** Quotes: "Switch off the spare node. What does insurance cost?" and "the spare-node switch in W-02 shows what it costs". **Problem:** The switch always adds exactly one empty node, whatever the setting, so the "cost" is always "one node". W-02 has no money amounts (mount contract: "no money amounts"). §05 presents spare room as a decision ("how much?"), but the widget offers only one fixed choice, so it can't show what the text says it shows. **Evidence:** `evaluate`: `nodes = base + (spare ? 1 : 0)` in every case. Defaults 5 → 4; at use 4 → 3; orders 12 → 5 → 4.

9. **Spare switch label.** Quotes: "Switch off the spare node" (s-04) and "the spare-node switch in W-02" (s-05). The widget's control reads "Keep room to lose a node" (`SHEET.spare`), with "On"/"Off". **Problem:** The text and the control use different names. A reader looking for a "spare node" switch finds a differently worded control. Only the drawn node says "Spare".

10. **W-02 node numbering compared with the story's nodes (s-01, s-03, s-04, k-05).** The widget numbers its packed nodes "Node 1 … n" (contract: "NODE · 1 … n"). The text talks about nodes 01, 02, 04, 05 and 06, with 03 dead. k-05, right after the widget, asks about "nodes 05 and 06". **Problem:** The widget draws a Node 3 and has no 05/06. Its nearly empty nodes are 4 and the spare (5). A reader may map widget nodes onto story nodes and get it wrong. Nothing in the text or the widget says the numbers are unrelated. **Evidence:** defaults, spare on: placement node 4 = [`no2`], node 5 = [] (spare).

11. **s-04, third "thing to notice".** Quote: "Push `orders` past 13 GB." **Problem:** Unlike the other two bullets, it gives no question or anything to look for. It also takes 15 clicks from 6 GB, with nothing changing until 13.5. Then the readouts disagree: "Used at the rush" still shows 19.5 GB, counting the unplaced orders Pod, while the idle bar's "Used at the rush" segment drops to 14 GB. The widget shows two different numbers under the same name. **Evidence:** (4, 13.5, 2), spare on → nodes null, `used` 19.5, `idle.used` 14, reserved 45.5. (4, 13, 2) → still 5 nodes.

12. **s-04, lead paragraph and first paragraph, compared with the widget's default.** Quotes: "Morgan's five nodes are the bill, and there are five because of the requests: on Friday, three nodes couldn't hold everything" and "counts the nodes those requests need … plus one spare node". **Problem:** The widget's default shows "05", the same number as Morgan's bill. Its 5 is 4 packed nodes plus a spare, while the real 5 are three original nodes plus two rented because spreading left a Pod pending. The text never says these are different 5s, or that tight packing needs 4 nodes for the Pods, which would explain why a fifth is "spare". **Evidence:** defaults → base 4, nodes 5. Placement node 4 holds only `no2` (2 GB), so tight packing still needs a fourth node for 2 GB of requests.
